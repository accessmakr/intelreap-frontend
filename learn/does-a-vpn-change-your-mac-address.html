Quick Answer

A VPN changes your IP, not your MAC

No. A VPN changes your public IP address, not your device's MAC address. A MAC address is used on your local network, so your router and other Wi-Fi users can see it, and a VPN does not hide it from them. Turn on your device's private or random Wi-Fi address to change what they see.

Open VPN and Proxy

A MAC address is the hardware address of a network adapter. It is used on a local link, the connection between your device and your router. RFC 826 defines how a device finds the hardware address that goes with an IP address on that link. The IP address is what travels across the internet to the website. This page is about the MAC (media access control) address, not an Apple Mac computer.

The two addresses do different jobs, which is why changing one does not change the other. What Is an IP Address? explains the internet side.

02 Who Can See Your MAC Address

Your router and the other devices on the same Wi-Fi or LAN can see your MAC address, because they communicate with it directly. A public hotspot operator can see it too. Apple's support page says a private Wi-Fi address reduces the ability of networks to track your device by a fixed address, which shows why networks care about it.

With a VPN on, the VPN server sees your real public IP address, and a website sees the VPN server's address. Neither sees your MAC on a normal IP tunnel. The Network Identity panel shows the public address a website sees for you.

03 What a VPN Changes and What It Does Not

A VPN changes the public IP address websites see and encrypts traffic between your device and the VPN server. It does not change your network adapter's MAC address, and it cannot hide that address from the local network, because the MAC is used on the link below the tunnel.

A VPN built on WireGuard or an OpenVPN tun interface works at the IP layer. WireGuard's whitepaper describes a layer 3 tunnel, and a tun interface carries IP packets, so no MAC crosses the tunnel. An OpenVPN tap interface works differently: its wiki describes bridging Ethernet frames, so the VPN adapter has its own virtual MAC. Neither type changes the MAC of your physical adapter.

A VPN can still reveal your real IP through other routes. The WebRTC leak test checks the best known one.

04 How to Hide Your MAC Address on Wi-Fi

To change the MAC a Wi-Fi network sees, use your device's private or random Wi-Fi address feature. It gives the network a random address instead of the hardware one.

Where to turn it on
PlatformSettingNotes
iPhone and iPad (iOS and iPadOS 14 and later)Settings, Wi-Fi, the info button next to the network, Private Wi-Fi AddressApple describes Fixed, which is the default for WPA2 and stronger networks, and Rotating, the default for open or weak ones. Older versions have a simple on or off switch.
Mac (macOS 14 and later)System Settings, Wi-Fi, Details next to the network, Private Wi-Fi AddressThe same options as on iPhone.

These settings cover Wi-Fi. Apple warns that some networks use the MAC for filtering or parental controls, which can stop working with a private address, and a Microsoft presentation to the IETF says networks that filter by MAC may not work with randomisation. Changing a hardware MAC by hand is possible on some systems, but it is outside this guide.

05 The IPv6 Exception

A website normally cannot read your MAC address, but IPv6 is a case to check. RFC 4941 explains that an IPv6 address can use an interface identifier derived from the MAC address, that this identifier is embedded in the address, and that privacy extensions generate changing identifiers instead. A device that builds addresses from its MAC would show that identifier to every site it contacts.

The second part of the check above looks for this pattern. It does not apply if your connection has no IPv6.

Logic

Separate the three places an address is used: the local link, where the MAC lives, the tunnel, where a VPN works, and the website, where only an IP normally arrives. Answer the question first, then show what a VPN changes, what actually changes the MAC, and the one case where a MAC can reach a website.

Methodology

Part 1 of the check reads your public IP, ISP, network owner, country, city and proxy and hosting flags from IntelReap's lookups, with and without your VPN, and compares the two readings kept in this tab. A web page cannot read a MAC address, so the check never shows it. Part 2 runs in your browser: it collects the public IPv6 addresses WebRTC reveals and tests for the ff:fe pattern of an address built from a MAC. That pattern appears by chance in about 1 in 65,536 random identifiers, so a match is a strong hint, not proof. It stores and sends nothing. The Apple and Android menu labels vary by version and maker, and the Apple default described for open networks is from Apple's page for recent versions. That your router, other devices on the network and a hotspot operator can see your MAC, and that a VPN server does not see it on an IP tunnel, is our reasoning from how local links and tunnels work. The Apple sentence on tracking is our paraphrase of Apple's description. We did not test the steps on real devices or test the VPN comparison with a real VPN.
