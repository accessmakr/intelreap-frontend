How Scanning Works

IntelReap uses standard browser APIs — including the Network Information API, WebRTC, WebGL, the Performance Observer, and navigator hardware properties — to read information your device already exposes to every website you visit. Most readings stay in your browser. To identify your network, your browser makes a few requests to our backend and to public IP detection services. They receive your public IP address and the standard headers every website receives, and the VPN and proxy check also sends your browser’s timezone and language along with the network and country already found for your IP. Your device, graphics, security and fingerprint readings are not sent.

Scoring Methodology

Your Global Intelligence Score weights nine dimensions: network, identity, privacy, device, graphics, security, capability, performance and speed. Security carries the heaviest weight at 20%, reflecting its outsized impact on real-world risk. Scores update live as each panel finishes analysis.

Reading Guide

How to read your results

A finished scan can show close to three hundred readings. You do not need to read them all. These six checks cover what most people want to know, in the order the panels appear.

1. Your address and your provider

Every connection has a public address, which works like a postal address for your network rather than for your device. The ISP and organization rows show who provides your access. The network panel shows which network owns the address, what type it is and how it connects to the wider internet. Many phones and home connections have two addresses, IPv4 and IPv6. When both are found, IntelReap shows the IPv4 address first and lists the IPv6 address as the other address. If you are new to the idea, start with what an IP address is or how to find the IP address of any device, then see the network route deep dive.

2. Why the location can be wrong, especially on mobile

A location database places your address using registration and routing records. It does not know where your phone is. Mobile providers send traffic through a small number of regional gateways, so your address can map to a city hundreds of kilometers away, and it can map somewhere different after you reconnect. The accuracy line shows the database’s own margin of error for that address, so treat the city as a rough guess. For your real position, the Fingerprinting panel has a “Compare with my GPS” button. Your browser asks for permission only when you tap it, and the position stays in your browser. More in the network identity and fingerprinting deep dives.

3. Are you hidden online?

The VPN and proxy panel compares several signals: whether the network belongs to a hosting company or a consumer provider, whether your browser’s timezone and language fit the address’s location, whether WebRTC reveals a different address, and whether the reverse DNS record agrees. A mismatch is a hint, not proof. Travel, language settings and mobile gateways can all cause one. “VPN detected: no” does not mean you are anonymous, and a clean trust score does not mean a leak is impossible. See the WebRTC leak test and how to protect your privacy online.

4. How trackable is your device

Websites can combine ordinary settings such as fonts, screen size, graphics rendering and audio behavior into a short code that recognizes a browser without cookies. The Fingerprinting panel shows those codes, which are computed in your browser and not sent anywhere. A higher fingerprinting surface means more distinctive signals are available to sites. Browsers with anti-fingerprinting modes and fewer extensions reduce it. Read what device fingerprinting is for how it works and how to limit it.

5. Device, graphics, security and speed

The device and graphics panels report what your browser says about your operating system, screen and graphics chip. The security panel checks whether the page uses HTTPS, whether WebRTC exposes a local address, and which permissions are granted, blocked or not yet requested. The performance and rendering panels time how quickly this page loaded and how fast your device runs short tests. These numbers move with battery saver, background apps and heat, so run the scan again before drawing conclusions. If graphics or WebGL show problems, see what a GPU is, how to enable WebGL and the GPU troubleshooting guide. For slow connections, read why internet speed fluctuates.

6. Reading the score

The global score is a weighted average of nine parts: security 20%, network 15%, device 15%, and capability, performance, speed and privacy 10% each, identity 5% and graphics 5%. It describes how well-configured and informative your environment looks, not how safe you are. Mobile connections, privacy-focused browsers and older devices can score lower on some parts without anything being wrong. Use the analysis line under each panel to see what, if anything, is worth changing.
