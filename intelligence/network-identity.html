Parameter by Parameter

Understanding Every Signal

What each value above actually means, and why it matters.

Network Identity

Public IP Address & IP Version

Your public IP is the address the rest of the internet sees when you connect, assigned by your provider and not fixed to your device. IP version tells you whether it uses the older IPv4 format or the newer IPv6 format. Many phones and home connections have both. When both are found, this panel shows the IPv4 address first and lists the IPv6 address as the other address.

ISP & Organization

Both rows show the name of the network that owns your address, taken from the same record, so they are normally identical. On corporate, university or VPN connections the company that owns the addresses can differ from the company that carries your traffic, and this panel cannot separate the two.

Country, Country Code, Region & City

These come from where a location database places your address, not from GPS. On mobile data the city can be hundreds of kilometers from you and can change when you reconnect, because carriers send traffic through a few regional gateways.

Postal Code & Coordinates

Coordinates are the point the database uses for your city, rounded to about one kilometer. The postal code row is usually empty: the database does not include postal codes, and the row fills only in the uncommon case where the network edge supplies one for your connection.

Latitude & Longitude

The numbers behind the map pin. They are derived from your IP address, not GPS. How far they can be from you ranges from a few kilometers on well-mapped fixed networks to hundreds of kilometers on mobile and satellite connections. The Location Accuracy row shows the database’s own margin for your address.

Timezone & UTC Offset

The timezone derived from your IP's registered location — not your device's system clock, which you could set to anything. A mismatch between this and the timezone your browser reports can be a signal that a VPN is routing your traffic through a different region. The UTC offset is the numeric version of the same information, useful for programmatic comparisons.

Connection Type & Mobile Connection

Connection type is one of four values: mobile, proxy, hosting or broadband. It is worked out from the same flags described in the next entry, so Mobile Connection simply repeats whether the mobile flag is set. Mobile connections matter because they almost always use carrier-grade NAT, so your public address is shared with many other subscribers.

Proxy Flag, Hosting Flag & Continent

These flags describe the network, not your device. A network is flagged as hosting when its number belongs to a datacenter or content-delivery operator, or its registered name matches a known hosting provider. It is flagged as proxy when its name matches a known VPN provider. Neither flag proves you are using a VPN: a hosting flag only means your connection exits through infrastructure that is also used for that purpose. Continent is context for systems that work at continental level.

Data Source

Shows where the location came from: a licensed IP location database stored with our backend or, where it has no record for your address, the network edge’s own data. Either way, the location is an estimate.

Other Address, Other Address Location, IPv6 Location (Database) & Location Accuracy

Other Address is the second address your device has, when one is found. Other Address Location and IPv6 Location (Database) show where the database places that second address, which can differ from the location of the address shown at the top. Location Accuracy is the database’s own margin of error for the address shown at the top: “within about 100 km” means the city is a rough guess, not a position.

Bigger Picture

What Your Network Identity Actually Exposes

Everything here is already visible to every site you visit

This panel doesn't collect anything unusual — it shows exactly what any web server already receives the moment your browser makes a request. Your public IP, ISP, and approximate location are visible to every website, API, and service you connect to, without any consent or action on your part. IntelReap just makes that information visible to you.

A VPN changes everything on this panel — and nothing on the fingerprinting panel

Connecting through a VPN replaces every value shown here with data reflecting your VPN exit server's location and ISP, not yours. That's exactly what VPNs are designed to do. What it doesn't change is your browser's fingerprint — the audio, canvas, and font signals measured in the Fingerprinting panel stay identical regardless of which network you're routed through.

Why the location can be wrong, especially on mobile

Location databases work from records about networks, not from your phone. A mobile network may route all of its customers in a region through one gateway, so your address is mapped to the gateway’s city, and a new session can be tied to a different gateway. Home Wi-Fi tends to be closer to the truth because fixed lines are registered to a service area.

What this panel cannot tell you

It cannot tell you your exact position, your device, or who you are. It describes the network your connection exits through. For your real position, the Fingerprinting panel offers a tap-only GPS comparison; for whether the pattern looks like a VPN, see the VPN & Proxy panel.
